# Reference: discovery question bank

Used when `source_artifacts` are thin and a founder/stakeholder Q&A is
possible (or when generating the closing questions for `DISCOVERY_GAPS.md`
per `SKILL.md` step 6). Organized by the six inventory dimensions from step
1. Not a script to run end-to-end — pull only the questions relevant to
covering a thin/empty dimension or closing a material gap.

## Business
- What does the business actually sell, in one sentence, without marketing
  language?
- How does it make money — one-time sale, subscription, service, other?
- How long has it operated, and has the offer changed materially since
  founding?
- What has to be true financially/operationally in the next 12 months for
  this to be considered a success?

## Offer
- What exactly is included/excluded in the offer? What's the price point,
  and how does that compare to alternatives customers might consider?
- What do customers actually get that a generic competitor doesn't?
- Is there a flagship product/service, or is the offer a spread of similar
  options?

## Customer
- Who buys today, in their own words if possible — not a demographic label,
  a description of the actual person/context?
- Who does the business *think* it serves vs. who the data shows actually
  buys — are they the same?
- What do customers say, unprompted, in reviews/support tickets/DMs? Pull
  verbatim language, not paraphrases.
- What's the actual moment/trigger that makes someone buy?

## Market
- Who are the 3-5 businesses a prospective customer would compare this to?
- What do those competitors do well that this business does not (or vice
  versa)?
- Is there a category convention (visual, verbal, pricing) this business
  follows, breaks, or hasn't decided on?

## Founder
- What does the founder personally want this brand to feel like, in their
  own words — not a category cliché?
- What would the founder refuse outright, even if it tested well (a
  non-negotiable)?
- What brand (any industry) does the founder point to and say "like that,"
  and *what specifically* about it do they mean — the look, the tone, the
  reputation, the business model?
- What brand does the founder explicitly not want to resemble, and why?

## Operational
- What's the actual budget and timeline for this brand work, and is either
  fixed or negotiable?
- Who has final approval authority on brand decisions?
- Are there legal/regulatory constraints on naming, claims, or imagery
  (e.g. medical/financial advertising rules)?
- Is there an existing brand asset (name, logo, domain, trademark) that must
  survive regardless of what discovery finds?

## Extracting references properly (pairs with SKILL.md step 4)
When a founder names a reference, always follow up (or infer only if the
material makes it unambiguous):
- "What specifically about [reference] — not the industry, the actual
  quality — do you mean?"
- "Is there a brand you actively don't want to be compared to, and what
  about it is the problem?"
Never record a reference name alone; the quality it signals is the useful
part.
