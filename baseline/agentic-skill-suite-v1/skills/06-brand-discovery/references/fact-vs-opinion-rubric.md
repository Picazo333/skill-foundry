# Reference: distinguishing FACTS from CLAIMS (and from ASPIRATIONS)

Used in `SKILL.md` step 2. This is the boundary discovery runs get wrong
most often — a founder states something with total confidence, and it gets
written into the brief as settled fact.

## The test
Ask of every extracted statement: **could someone other than the person who
said it verify it, right now, without asking them again?**

- **Yes, and it's about the present/past** → FACTS. ("We have 3 locations."
  "We were founded in 2019." "Average order value is $42." A direct
  customer quote from a review is a FACT that the quote exists and says
  that — even if the opinion inside the quote is subjective, the fact of
  the utterance is verifiable.)
- **No — it's an unverified assertion about standing, quality, or
  perception, stated as if settled** → CLAIM. ("We're the most trusted
  clinic in the city." "Our customers are obsessed with us." "We're already
  known for X.") Record who said it. A claim can still be *true* — the
  point is not that it's false, it's that discovery has not verified it, so
  it must not be handed to `brand-strategy` disguised as fact.
- **No — because it's explicitly about a future/desired state, not a
  present one** → ASPIRATION. ("We want to feel like the Aesop of dental
  care." "We want people to see us as premium.") The giveaway is
  future-tense or want/wish framing.
- **No — nobody said it, the skill inferred it from a pattern in the
  material** → HYPOTHESIS, always tagged unconfirmed.

## Common failure patterns
- **A confident tone is not evidence.** Founders often state CLAIMS with the
  same certainty as FACTS. Certainty of delivery is not a signal for
  classification — verifiability is.
- **"Everyone knows we're the best X" is a CLAIM, not market research.** If
  no analytics/survey/review data backs it, it stays a CLAIM even if
  repeated by multiple stakeholders — multiple people asserting the same
  unverified thing is still zero independent verification.
- **A stat with no source is a CLAIM about a stat, not a FACT.** "We have a
  90% retention rate" without a source document or data pull is a CLAIM
  until the underlying data is supplied.
- **Aspiration bleeding into current-perception.** Watch for a stakeholder
  describing the desired future state while grammatically describing the
  present ("we're the friendly neighborhood option" when evidence suggests
  otherwise) — classify by what's actually true today per available
  evidence (current perception axis), not by the tense used.
- **Do not "average" a CLAIM into a FACT by cross-referencing two
  stakeholders who agree.** Two unverified assertions agreeing with each
  other raises confidence in the claim but does not make it a fact; note
  the corroboration in the CLAIM entry, keep it a CLAIM.

## When it's genuinely ambiguous
If a statement could reasonably be FACT or CLAIM depending on unstated
context (e.g. "we're profitable" — could be a P&L fact or a vague founder
assertion), classify it as CLAIM and note in the entry what would confirm it
as fact ("would need: latest P&L"). Default to the more conservative bucket
— it is always safer to under-claim certainty than to hand `brand-strategy`
a false FACT.
