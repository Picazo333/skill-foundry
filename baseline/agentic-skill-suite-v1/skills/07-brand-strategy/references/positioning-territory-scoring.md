# Reference: scoring positioning territories

Used in `SKILL.md` step 5 (Generate positioning territories) and step 6
(Recommend and document rejection).

## The five axes

Score each territory 1–5 on each axis. A score without a cited piece of
evidence or a stated constraint is not a score — it's a guess, and must not
be entered.

### Distinctiveness
How different is this territory from how competitors already position
themselves (per the competitive frame from step 4)?
- 1 — indistinguishable from at least one direct competitor's actual claim.
- 3 — a different emphasis on a shared theme competitors also gesture at.
- 5 — stakes a claim no competitor in the frame is credibly making.

### Credibility
Can the brand actually back this claim with real proof (RTBs), today or
with a committed near-term change — not aspirationally?
- 1 — no evidence in the discovery brief supports it; would require
  fabrication.
- 3 — partially supported; some proof exists, some would need to be built.
- 5 — directly supported by specific, cited discovery-brief facts.

### Relevance
Does this territory address something the audience (per step 3) evidently
cares about?
- 1 — no audience evidence connects to this territory's core claim.
- 3 — plausible relevance, inferred rather than evidenced.
- 5 — directly evidenced by audience/VoC data.

### Extensibility
Can this territory hold up across the brand's likely future offerings/
category moves, or does it lock the brand into one narrow claim?
- 1 — only makes sense for the current single offering.
- 3 — extends to adjacent offerings with some strain.
- 5 — extends cleanly across the business model's likely trajectory.

### Business fit
Does this territory fit the business model, cost structure, and any hard
constraints from step 2?
- 1 — contradicts a stated constraint.
- 3 — workable but creates tension with a business reality.
- 5 — reinforces the business model rather than fighting it.

## How to use the scores

1. Generate 3–5 territories that make genuinely different bets — if two
   territories would use the same reasons-to-believe, they are not
   distinct; merge or replace one.
2. Score every territory on all five axes before recommending any of them.
   Scoring after picking a favorite invites motivated reasoning.
3. The recommendation is the highest total that no constraint blocks —
   not the highest distinctiveness alone (a wildly distinctive but
   incredible or irrelevant claim is not a usable strategy) and not the
   safest average (a territory that scores evenly-mediocre on everything is
   usually the commoditized one — check it against
   `references/category-and-commoditization-test.md` before recommending
   it).
4. Every territory that is not recommended goes into
   `POSITIONING_SYSTEM.md`'s discarded-alternatives table with its specific
   lowest-scoring axis named as the reason — not a generic dismissal.
