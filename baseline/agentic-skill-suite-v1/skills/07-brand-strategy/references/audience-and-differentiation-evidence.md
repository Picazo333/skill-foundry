# Reference: what counts as evidence for audience and differentiation claims

Used in `SKILL.md` step 3 (audience/ICP definition) and step 6
(recommending a territory) — the evidence bar that keeps both from becoming
fiction.

## What counts as evidence for an audience claim

- **Primary research** — interviews, surveys, sales-call notes, support
  tickets, usage/behavioral data — attributable to real people the business
  has actually talked to or observed.
- **Observed behavior** — what the audience does today (current tool/
  workaround, switching history, purchase pattern), not what they say they
  would do.
- **Direct quotes or VoC** — verbatim language from the discovery brief's
  voice-of-customer material, not a paraphrase that smooths over what was
  actually said.
- **Market proxy** — a named, cited comparable (a competitor's stated
  customer base, a category report) used explicitly as a proxy, labeled as
  such, not folded silently into the brand's own audience description.

## What does not count

- The founder's intuition about who the audience "really" is, stated
  without a source.
- An analogy to a competitor's audience ("basically like [Competitor]'s
  customers") presented as this brand's own evidence.
- Aspiration — who the business *wants* to attract next, as opposed to who
  it evidently serves today.
- A demographic or psychographic detail invented to make a persona feel
  complete (an age range, a lifestyle detail, a stated value) that traces to
  no source in the discovery brief. This is the single most common way
  strategy work quietly becomes fiction — a specific, invented detail reads
  as more credible than an honest gap, which is exactly the problem.

## The differentiation test

Before a claimed point of difference goes into `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` as
established (not just proposed), it must pass all three:

1. **True** — traceable to a specific fact in the discovery brief or a
   committed near-term change, not an aspiration stated in the present
   tense.
2. **Relevant** — connects to something the audience (per step 3, built to
   the evidence bar above) evidently cares about — not merely something the
   brand is proud of.
3. **Not identically reclaimable** — at least one named direct competitor
   from the competitive frame (step 4) could not credibly make the same
   claim in the same words. If two or more competitors could swap in for
   the brand name and the claim would still read as true, it is not
   differentiation — run the commoditization swap test in
   `references/category-and-commoditization-test.md` before proceeding.

A claim that fails any of the three is not disqualified from the run — it
is downgraded to a hypothesis, named as such, and logged in
`BRAND_STRATEGY.md`'s unresolved/assumptions section. It does not enter the
recommended positioning as fact.

## When evidence is thin

Thin evidence is a normal, expected state — not a blocker and not license to
fill the gap:

- State the specific attribute or claim that is under-evidenced.
- State the confidence level explicitly (e.g. "inferred from a single
  support-ticket theme, not confirmed by interview").
- Mark it as an assumption requiring validation, not as a locked fact —
  this is what downstream skills (`brand-verbal-identity`,
  `brand-visual-direction`) rely on to know which parts of the strategy are
  load-bearing and which are provisional.
- Never substitute a specific invented detail for an honest "unknown" —
  see the fictional-persona failure mode this skill's `SKILL.md` describes.
