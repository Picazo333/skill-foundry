**VERBAL_IDENTITY.md** (excerpt):

### 1. Say "gone" like it's a fact, not a hook
Traces to: differentiator — no restock, ever, 3-year track record
DO: "Sold out. Not coming back. That's the whole point."
DON'T: "Sold out?! Don't miss the restock — sign up for early access!"

### 2. Undersell the object, oversell nothing
Traces to: audience — "rather own something than be seen owning something"
DO: "Heavyweight fleece. Runs true to size. That's it."
DON'T: "The hoodie you NEED this season. Elevated streetwear essential."
```

**MESSAGE_HIERARCHY.md** (excerpt):
```
Pillar 1: No restock is the product, not a scarcity tactic
Proof point: zero restocks across 3 years and every SKU shipped
One line: "We don't do restocks. We do runs of 200 and then we move on."

Tagline territory: "Own it, don't announce it"
Sample directions: "Not for the feed." / "200 made. None coming back."
```

**VOICE_EXAMPLES.md** (excerpt, principle 1, format: social caption):
```
DO: "That's it. Gone. On to the next one."
DON'T: "OMG these SOLD OUT in minutes 🔥🔥🔥 restock coming soon, stay tuned!!"
Stress test note: nearly every competitor streetwear brand's whole business

Note what did NOT happen: the skill did not default to generic hype-culture
streetwear voice ("fire drop," "don't sleep") — the strategy's specific
anti-hype, no-restock differentiator drove principles that would actively
contradict a typical competitor's incentives.
