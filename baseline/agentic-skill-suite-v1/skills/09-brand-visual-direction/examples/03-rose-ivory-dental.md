# Worked Example — Rose/Ivory Dental Identity

## Input (abbreviated `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`)
- **Category:** Boutique dental practice (cosmetic + general), independent,
  competing against both corporate DSO chains (generic, clinical-white,
  teal-accent branding) and other boutique practices already using
  soft-pastel "spa dentistry" visual codes.
- **Positioning:** "Care that looks like you're a person, not a chart" —
  differentiation is relational warmth without tipping into the "spa
  dentistry" cliché that most boutique competitors already occupy.
- **Audience:** Adults who avoid dental visits due to clinical coldness or
  past bad experiences; value being treated as an individual.
- **Constraint supplied:** Client explicitly requested rose/ivory as a
  palette starting point; anti-reference supplied: "not another spa-tooth
  brand — every other boutique practice in town already looks like a
  candle store."

## Step 1 — tension derivation (excerpt)
| Attribute | Tension | Rules out |
|---|---|---|
| "Person, not a chart" | Individual warmth vs. clinical genericism | Generic clinical-white + teal DSO look; also rules out the *other* generic — indistinct pastel "spa" sameness |
| Rose/ivory constraint vs. "not another spa-tooth brand" | Palette given, but *how it's used* must differ from the local category default | A soft, evenly-diffused rose wash applied uniformly (the exact thing every competitor already does) |

Note: the client-supplied palette (rose/ivory) is a **constraint**, not a
territory-differentiator by itself — with a fixed palette, the 8 axes
other than color behavior must carry the differentiation, and color
*behavior* (not the hues) still has room to differ from the local default.

## Territories generated (summary)
1. **"Portrait Practice"** (selected) — rose/ivory used as a restrained,
   asymmetric accent (not an evenly diffused wash) against a warm-neutral
   base; editorial portrait photography of actual practice staff/patients
   (with consent) rather than generic stock-smile imagery; type logic
   pairs a confident structural sans with a handwritten-adjacent signature
   mark used sparingly (for the practice name only, not body text);
   composition uses asymmetric, magazine-editorial layout rather than
   centered/symmetrical "calm spa" layout.
2. **"Warm Clinical"** (runner-up) — leans further clinical-precise
   (structured grid, higher color restraint, rose used only as a single
   functional accent) — diverges from Territory 1 on composition and
   density; scored lower on strategic fit (reads slightly too corporate
   for "person, not a chart").
3. **"Soft Bloom"** (discarded — this is the local category default:
   evenly-diffused rose wash, centered symmetrical layout, generic
   soft-focus smile photography; flagged immediately against the
   "spa dentistry" anti-reference and the wellness-category cliché
   checklist; kept in the file only as the explicit example of what was
   ruled out, not as a live option).

## Selection
Territory 1, "Portrait Practice" — satisfies the client's palette
constraint while resolving the anti-reference through composition, image
language, and type logic rather than color, which is exactly where this
category's competitors are NOT differentiating.

## Output excerpt — `VISUAL_ANTI_PATTERNS.md`
> Ruled out: evenly-diffused all-over rose/pastel wash as primary
> background treatment — this is the exact "spa dentistry" look named in
> the client's anti-reference and matched by every boutique competitor
> surveyed.
> Ruled out: generic stock photography of anonymous smiling models —
> contradicts "person, not a chart" directly; imagery must be of real
> practice staff/patients or none at all.
