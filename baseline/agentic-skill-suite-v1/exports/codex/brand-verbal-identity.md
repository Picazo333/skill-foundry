# Brand Verbal Identity

> Canonical skill definition. Adapters in `adapters/` are thin wrappers over
> this file — they may not change purpose, procedure, outputs, quality gates
> or stop conditions. See `/PORTABILITY.md` and `/shared/adapters/README.md`.

## Identity
- **ID:** `brand-verbal-identity`
- **Category:** brand
- **Version:** `1.0.0`
- **Purpose:** Convert a locked brand strategy into an operational verbal
  identity system — behavioral voice principles, tone modulation by context,
  vocabulary rules, a message hierarchy, and DO/DON'T examples — precise
  enough that a writer who never met the founder can reproduce the voice
  correctly.

## Trigger
Use this skill when:
- `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` exists with a locked positioning statement and value
  proposition.
- Multiple writers/agents/channels need repeatable voice and copy
  consistency, not one-off phrasing.
- The brand workflow has reached the strategy → verbal/visual fork (see
  `/WORKFLOW_MAP.md`) and verbal identity is needed alongside, or ahead of,
  `brand-visual-direction`.

## Non-trigger
Do not use this skill for:
- Long-form content strategy (editorial calendar, content pillar execution,
  channel cadence) — that is `brand-content-system`.
- The final visual system (color, type, imagery, layout) — that is
  `brand-visual-direction` / `brand-identity-system`. Verbal identity must
  not wait on or depend on visual decisions.
- A single piece of one-off copy with no reusable need — just write the copy
  directly; there is no artifact system to build.
- A project with no `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` yet — route to `brand-strategy`
  first. Deriving voice from vibes instead of positioning is exactly the
  failure this skill exists to prevent.

## Context policy
- **REQUIRED_CONTEXT:** `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`, and specifically the positioning
  statement, value proposition, target audience, and at least one stated
  differentiator or proof point inside it.
- **OPTIONAL_CONTEXT:** existing copy samples, customer language (reviews,
  support transcripts, sales call notes), compliance/legal/regulatory
  constraints, the target channel list, brand-discovery artifacts.
- **DO_NOT_LOAD_BY_DEFAULT:** full conversation/chat history; unrelated
  skills' canon (product audits, implementation plans); the visual identity
  system's color/type/imagery decisions — a voice tied to a logo color is a
  sign the derivation went wrong; raw founder speech/writing samples treated
  as a template to imitate (optional as *signal* about audience relationship
  only, never as a style to copy — see Failure modes).

Prefer canonical artifacts over conversational memory (`ARCHITECTURE.md`).

## Procedure
Numbered, executable steps. Each step should be concrete enough that two
independent runs against the same `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` converge on materially
the same voice principles and message hierarchy — not just "on brand" copy
that varies wildly between runs.

1. **Validate strategy sufficiency (gate).** Confirm `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`
   contains: a positioning statement, a value proposition, a target
   audience, and at least one named differentiator or proof point. If any of
   these is missing or so vague it can't anchor a decision (e.g. "we care
   about quality" with no specifics), **stop and return `BLOCKED`** — do not
   proceed to derive a voice from adjectives alone. This gate exists
   specifically to prevent generic "innovative, bold, authentic" output.
2. **Extract voice-relevant signal.** From the strategy (and optional
   inputs), pull: what the brand is *for* versus competitors, how it wants
   the audience to feel treated (peer vs. authority vs. guide), category
   norms it must observe (e.g. regulated claims) or deliberately break from,
   and concrete proof points/differentiators. Tag each extracted signal with
   its source line in `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`.
3. **Derive 3–5 behavioral voice principles.** Each principle is a rule
   about what the brand *does* in language, not an adjective — see
   `references/tone-of-voice-dimension-framework.md` for how to convert a
   dimension position into a behavioral rule, and
   `references/writing-do-dont-examples.md` for the required format. Each
   principle must (a) trace to a specific signal from step 2, and (b) come
   with at least one DO and one DON'T example drafted immediately, not
   deferred to step 7. A principle that cannot be traced to strategy is
   dropped, not kept as "nice to have."
4. **Define tone modulation by context.** For each of: sales, support,
   education, social, crisis/apology, and product/UI copy — state what stays
   constant (the behavioral principles) and what dials up/down (formality,
   warmth, brevity, humor). Crisis/apology context always dials humor and
   irreverence toward zero regardless of the brand's default position.
5. **Build vocabulary rules.** Three lists: preferred terms/phrasings
   (words the brand reaches for), avoided terms (off-brand but not
   dangerous), and prohibited claims (legally/factually unsafe — required
   whenever compliance constraints are supplied or the category demands it,
   e.g. health, finance, legal, safety). An empty prohibited-claims list is
   only valid when no compliance constraint applies and the category isn't
   regulated; otherwise it's a gate failure (see Quality gates).
6. **Build the message hierarchy.** One master message; 3–5 pillars, each
   with a supporting proof point traced to strategy; one elevator pitch
   (≈2 sentences); descriptions at three lengths (one-liner, ~25 words,
   ~75 words); 2–3 tagline *territories* (directions, not finished taglines
   — finishing a tagline is a copywriting decision outside this skill's
   scope). Every pillar must map 1:1 to a differentiator or proof point in
   `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` — no invented pillar.
7. **Write DO/DON'T examples across channels.** For each voice principle,
   produce at least one DO/DON'T pair in each of at least 4 formats (e.g.
   homepage headline, support reply, social caption, error/empty-state
   copy, sales one-liner) — enough breadth that a writer sees the principle
   applied, not just described.
8. **Define syntax/rhythm/complexity conventions** where the audience or
   category makes them load-bearing: sentence length bias, contraction use,
   jargon tolerance, reading-level target, sentence-fragment tolerance,
   numerals vs. spelled-out numbers. Skip this section explicitly (state why)
   rather than filling it with defaults when it isn't load-bearing.
9. **Stress-test.** Take the DO examples from step 7 and check: could a
   direct competitor's writer have produced this same line without
   contradicting *their* strategy? If yes for a given principle, that
   principle is still too generic — return to step 3 and sharpen it against
   the strategy signal. Run this check across at least 4 channels before
   proceeding.
10. **Stop.** Complete when a writer or agent unfamiliar with any specific
    person's cadence could apply `VERBAL_IDENTITY.md` + `VOICE_EXAMPLES.md`
    correctly with no founder-imitation prompt needed (see Failure modes).

### Checkpoints
For a `STANDARD` or `DEEP` run, checkpoint after step 3 (voice principles
locked, each traced + DO/DON'T drafted) and after step 6 (message hierarchy
built, before the full multi-channel example set in step 7). A resumed run
re-reads the checkpointed principles/hierarchy rather than re-deriving them
from `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` again, unless the strategy itself changed
(`mode: UPDATE`).

## Outputs

### Required output artifacts
- `VERBAL_IDENTITY.md` — behavioral voice principles (each traced to
  strategy, each with DO/DON'T), tone modulation matrix by context,
  vocabulary rules (preferred/avoided/prohibited), syntax/rhythm
  conventions.
- `MESSAGE_HIERARCHY.md` — master message, pillars with proof, elevator
  pitch, descriptions at three lengths, tagline territories.
- `VOICE_EXAMPLES.md` — DO/DON'T examples per principle across ≥4 channels/
  formats, stress-tested against the generic-competitor check.

## Failure modes
Concrete ways this skill can go wrong, and what to do instead:
- **Adjective-only voice ("innovative, bold, authentic").** Detect: a
  principle in `VERBAL_IDENTITY.md` is a bare adjective with no behavioral
  rule and no traceable link to a `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` line. Fix: rederive
  from step 2's signal, or if the strategy genuinely has no traceable
  signal to derive from, return `BLOCKED` instead of publishing filler.
- **Founder-imitation dependency.** Detect: a DO example only reads
  correctly when written in one specific person's idiosyncratic cadence, or
  the principle is phrased as "sounds like [founder]" instead of a
  reproducible rule. Fix: rewrite the principle and examples as a rule
  anyone could apply; founder speech samples are signal for step 2, never a
  style template.
- **Missing DO/DON'T pairing.** Detect: a principle or vocabulary rule is
  stated with no observable boundary example. Fix: add before treating the
  run as complete — an unillustrated boundary isn't operational.
- **Silently dropping compliance constraints.** Detect: compliance
  constraints were supplied (or the category is regulated) but
  `VERBAL_IDENTITY.md` has no prohibited-claims section. Fix: block
  completion until it's added; when in doubt about a specific claim's
  legality, mark it `NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW` rather than guessing.
- **Message hierarchy invents pillars.** Detect: a pillar in
  `MESSAGE_HIERARCHY.md` has no corresponding differentiator/proof point in
  `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`. Fix: cut the pillar or send it back as an unresolved
  item for `brand-strategy` to confirm — this skill does not invent
  positioning.
- **Voice indistinguishable from a direct competitor's.** Detect: step 9's
  stress test passes a DO example that a competitor's writer could equally
  have produced without contradicting their own strategy. Fix: sharpen the
  principle against the specific differentiator it's supposed to encode.

## Quality gates
Skill must satisfy `/QUALITY_GATES.md` skill-level gates 1–10, plus:
- Every voice principle is behavioral (a rule, not an adjective) and traces
  to a specific line in `BRAND_STRATEGY.md`.
- Every voice principle has at least one DO and one DON'T example in
  `VOICE_EXAMPLES.md`.
- A prohibited-claims section exists in `VERBAL_IDENTITY.md` whenever
  compliance constraints were supplied or the category is regulated.
- Every pillar in `MESSAGE_HIERARCHY.md` maps 1:1 to a strategy
  differentiator or proof point — none invented.
- The voice is not dependent on founder imitation — a reviewer unfamiliar
  with any specific person could apply it correctly from the artifacts
  alone.

## Stop conditions
- Normal completion: all three required artifacts produced, step 9's
  stress test run across ≥4 channels with no failing principle,
  `status: COMPLETE`.
- Blocked (insufficient input): `BRAND_STRATEGY.md` is missing a positioning
  statement, value proposition, target audience, or any traceable
  differentiator → `status: BLOCKED`, state exactly which element is
  missing and that generic filler will not be produced in its place.
- Never continue past: publishing voice principles that are bare adjectives
  with no DO/DON'T pairing and no traceable link to strategy — that is the
  AI-slop failure this skill exists to prevent, and it fails the skill's
  purpose even if the run otherwise "completes."
